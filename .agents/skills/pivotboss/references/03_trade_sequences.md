# Step 2b: Trade Sequences (PDF pp. 32–49)

A Trade Sequence links several day types in a row that together fuel the next move. All are written bullish except Failed Absorption, which is written bearish ("*flip*" for the other side).

---

## 1. Rejection Day Sequence (pp.32–36)
*Also applies to any major rejection day, including **Failed New Low** and **Outside Day**.*

| # | Stage | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rejection Day** | Open a full or partial position on the confirmed rejection day (last hour), or later at the Primary Absorption Zone |
| 2 | **Primary Absorption Zone** | Zone between the Rejection MID and the rejected price. A **daily close below it ruins the trade** (stop run down), so exit |
| 3 | **Absorption Day(s)** | Add, or open a new position, at the zone. **Price must open above the Rejection MID** for the trigger to count. Usually lasts 1–4 days |
| 4 | **Failed New Low** | Add or open on an FNL, usually 2–3 days after the rejection (must re-establish acceptance above yLO) |
| 5 | **Stop Run Day** | Add at the breakout point or lower. **Take partial profits ahead of the close** |
| 6 | **Secondary Absorption Zone** | Zone between the SRD MID and recent resistance. Defend or open here. A daily close below it means exit |
| 7 | **Retest After Stop Run** | Add or open on a retest of prior resistance from above |
| 8 | **Continuation Day** | Moves price to the primary target. Take partial profits and reduce risk |

**Goal:** take profits on the first favorable pop into the target zone, and exit if price fails to hold the absorption zone on a daily closing basis.

## 2. Stop Run Sequence (pp.37–41)
| # | Stage | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Stop Run Day** | Earliest entry is an FNL / rejection of yLO (including retests from above). Second entry is expansion through the breakout point (previous highs / yHI). Take partial profits before the close |
| 2 | **Primary Absorption Zone** | Between the SRD MID and the rejected level. A daily close below it ruins the sequence, so exit |
| 3 | **Fade After Stop Run** | A rejection at the SRD high gives a countertrend fade back to the SRD MID. Use it as a standalone day trade or a **hedge** |
| 4 | **Absorption Days** | Add or open, including on days with Failed New Lows (Day 3 often shows an FNL) |
| 5 | **Continuation Day** | Moves to the primary target. Take partial profits |

**Fade options for Day 2 (p.39):**
1. Standalone short on rejection at yHI, target yMID
2. Short hedge to protect the long position
3. Scale part of the long in the last hour of the SRD, then add back at the absorption zone on Day 2

Bulls generally need to keep the SRD MID bid. Pullbacks to the zone over the next 2–3 days are entry or defense opportunities.

## 3. Failed Absorption Sequence (pp.42–46), written bearish
| # | Stage | Description |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rejection Day** | Bulls build longs at the Primary Absorption Zone. If they don't get paid, sell stops trigger through the zone |
| 2 | **Primary Absorption Zone** | Between the Rejection MID and the rejected price. Bulls defend it; bears target the stops below |
| 3 | **Absorption Days** | Bulls defend, but after **3–5 days of failing effort** a Stop Run Day may be imminent. 2–5 days of absorption builds enough energy |
| 4 | **Failed Breakout** | After a **daily close below the Rejection MID**, bulls try to expand above yHI and fail. **Earliest short entry**: at the rejected level / rejection or retest of yHI the next morning |
| 5 | **Stop Run Day** | The failed breakout triggers sell stops and an aggressive SRD down. Add on a retest of yLO from below. Take partial profits in the last hour |
| 6 | **Retest After Stop Run** | Add or open on a retest of the failed absorption zone from below, or at the SRD midpoint the next morning. **New absorption zone** = SRD MID ↔ the 2-day absorption lows. A daily close back above the primary absorption zone ruins it, so exit |
| 7 | **Continuation Day** | Moves to the primary target. Take partial profits |

Trigger: failure. Price fails to go higher and participants don't get paid, which leads to long liquidation. Watch for failed breakouts and failed new highs when a bullish rejection sequence "can't find liftoff".

## 4. Accumulation Sequence (pp.47–49)
| # | Stage | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Accumulation** | Institutions buy supply while keeping price in a **narrow range for 20+ days**. **Rounded bottoms** are very reliable. Range compression in the final weeks hints at expansion |
| 2 | **Breakout / Absorption Zone** | Between the SRD MID and recent resistance. A daily close below it means exit |
| 3 | **Rejection Day** | A rejection on the **right side of a rounded bottom** often comes before expansion. Open a partial position that day, or at the Rejection MID next session |
| 4 | **Absorption Day** | Add or open at the zone |
| 5 | **Stop Run Day** | Add, or wait for the retest (6) |
| 6 | **Retest After Stop Run** | Defend or open on a retest of the breakout point from above. Multiple rotations can be defended. A daily close below it means exit |
| 7 | **Continuation Day** | Take partial profits, or hold for 6–12 month targets / buy the dips |

**Managing the trade (p.49)**
- **Time-based target:** the markup phase lasts about as long as the accumulation phase or longer (8 weeks accumulation → roughly 6–8 weeks of expansion).
- The transition from accumulation to markup should be quick and aggressive.
- Once in markup, every pullback to the **PEMA trigger zone** (8/21 EMA) is a buyable dip.
- Trim along the way. Exit the remainder at the price target, at the time target, or when price becomes range-bound.
